Starting rosuvastatin 5 mg after side effects with a statin

Information and guidance for patients and carers in England

Rosuvastatin is a statin. It lowers cholesterol and can help reduce your chance of a heart attack or stroke. If you’ve had side effects with a statin before, your prescriber may suggest starting rosuvastatin on just three days a week to see how you get on. (NHS: Rosuvastatin; NHS England: Statin intolerance pathway)

Your four-week starting plan

Follow this schedule only if the healthcare professional who prescribed rosuvastatin has agreed it with you. If the instructions on your medicine label are different, check with your prescriber or pharmacist before taking it.

Weeks 1 to 4
Take one 5 mg tablet on Monday, Wednesday and Friday only. Do not take it on the other four days.
From week 5
If you’ve had no new or returning side effects, take one 5 mg tablet every day, including weekends. The tablet strength stays the same; only the number of days changes.

If side effects start or come back, or if you’re not sure whether you should switch to daily tablets, don’t increase how often you take it. Ask your prescriber what to do next. They can help you find a schedule or a different treatment that suits you. (NICE: What to do if a statin causes side effects)

Taking rosuvastatin three days a week is outside the usual licensed instructions, which is sometimes called off-label use. This is an option a prescriber may choose for some people who have had trouble taking statins every day; this exact four-week schedule is not a rule for everyone. (South West London NHS: Lipid management guidance)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take my tablet with food?

With or without food: Take your tablet at around the same time on the days it’s due. A phone reminder can help, especially while you’re taking it only three days a week. (NHS: How to take rosuvastatin)

What if I miss a dose?

Skip it and take your next tablet on your next planned day. Don’t take two tablets to make up for a missed dose. (NHS: Missed doses)

When do I need a follow-up blood test?

Ask your GP practice when to book your next blood test. It’s usually due 2 to 3 months after starting or changing treatment, to check your cholesterol and look for any effect on your liver. (NICE: Blood tests after starting or changing treatment)

When to ask for help

  • Contact your prescriber promptly if you get muscle pain, tenderness or weakness you can’t explain. Don’t wait until the end of the four weeks to report it. (NICE: Advice on muscle symptoms)
  • Call NHS 111 for urgent advice if you have severe muscle pain or weakness, especially if you also have a fever or your pee looks red or brown. These can be signs of a rare but serious side effect. (NHS: Rosuvastatin side effects)
  • Call 999 if your lips, tongue or throat suddenly swell, or you’re struggling to breathe. These can be signs of a serious allergic reaction. (NHS: Anaphylaxis)

Free support from your pharmacy

If rosuvastatin is new to you and you live in England, you may be able to get free support through the NHS New Medicine Service (NMS). Ask your pharmacist if you can take part when you collect your first prescription.

After a chat when you collect your medicine, your pharmacist will usually check in 7 to 14 days later, then again 2 to 3 weeks after that; you can speak by phone or in a private room at the pharmacy. You can talk about how you’re getting on, any side effects, and ways to remember which days to take your tablets. If needed, the pharmacist can refer you back to your prescriber. (New Medicine Service patient leaflet; NHS England: New Medicine Service)

You don’t need to wait for your pharmacy appointment if you’re worried about side effects. Follow the advice in “When to ask for help” above, and check with your prescriber if you’re unsure about switching to daily tablets.
